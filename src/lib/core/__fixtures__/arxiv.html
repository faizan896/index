Title:Sparse Mixture Routing for Long-Context Retrieval

Authors:Chidi Okafor, Maja Lindqvist, Hiroshi Tanaka, Élise Moreau, Ravi Patel
View a PDF of the paper titled Sparse Mixture Routing for Long-Context Retrieval, by Chidi Okafor and 4 other authors
View PDF HTML (experimental)
Abstract:Retrieval-augmented language models struggle when the evidence for an answer is spread across documents far longer than their context window. Dense retrievers trained on short passages tend to return near-duplicates of the same evidence, while sparse retrievers miss paraphrases. We introduce Sparse Mixture Routing (SMR), a retrieval layer that assigns each query to a small set of specialised retrievers and merges their results with a learned gate. The router is trained with a contrastive objective that rewards retrieving complementary rather than redundant passages, and adds less than two milliseconds of latency per query. On four long-document question answering benchmarks, SMR improves exact match by 6.1 points on average over dense retrieval baselines while reading 40% fewer passages. Gains are largest on questions whose answers require combining evidence from distant sections of a document. We analyse which routing decisions matter most, show that the learned gate transfers to unseen domains without retraining, and release code and checkpoints.
Comments: 14 pages, 6 figures. Accepted to ACL 2024
